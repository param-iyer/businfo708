Linear Programming (LP) Fundamentals

What is Linear Programming?

Linear Programming is a mathematical modeling technique used to allocate limited resources optimally among competing activities. In our espresso roaster lab, the objective is to maximize the **Total Operational Profit function ($Z$)**:

Maximize: Z = P₁x₁ + P₂x₂

where $x_1$ represents bags of Light Roast coffee ($6 profit) and $x_2$ represents bags of Medium Roast coffee ($8 profit).

The Extreme Point Theorem

The fundamental theorem of linear programming states that if an optimal solution exists for a bounded feasible region, it **must occur at one of the corner points (vertices)** of that region! This is why the legendary **Simplex Algorithm** works—it traverses corner points sequentially, finding the optimal vertex in minimal steps.

Coffee Shop Limits (Constraints)

Our coffee shop is bound by physical operations parameters:

  • Kiln Resource Constraint: Roasting beans requires heating energy. Light Roast takes 12 units of kiln time, and Medium Roast takes 18. 12x₁ + 18x₂ ≤ 3000
  • Raw Bean Constraint: Supply stock is limited. Medium Roast takes slightly more green beans to roast deeper. 1.00x₁ + 1.05x₂ ≤ 205
  • Labour Constraint: Operational staffing hours. Light Roast takes 2.0 hours of craft labour per bag, and Medium Roast takes 3.0. 2.00x₁ + 3.00x₂ ≤ 500
